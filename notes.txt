The LLDM engine is a decoupled, event-driven tabletop RPG framework. It strictly separates mechanical state from narrative presentation,
using a central Pub/Sub message broker to route information between specialized apps.

Architecture and Event Staging
The system is divided into a Core App, Data App, AI App, and Client App. These communicate through the Event Bus.
To prevent the user interface from spoiling the story, the Event Bus uses a staging queue.
The Core App calculates mechanical outcomes instantly and sends the raw data to the AI App.
The Client App UI, including health and resource tracks, does not update until the AI App broadcasts a NARRATIVE_COMPLETE event containing the generated text.


Input Handling
Player input is processed by an NLP Bridge using text embeddings and semantic similarity.
The player's raw text is mathematically compared against a hardcoded list of standard engine actions
(e.g., Melee Attack, Ranged Attack, Cast Spell) via cosine similarity.
If the highest match meets a strict confidence threshold, the engine extracts the intent and executes the action.
If the score falls below the threshold, the engine halts and prompts the player to clarify. This deterministic approach prevents hallucinated actions.


Core Stats and Combat (D6 System)
Attributes, resistance, and damage pools are measured in 6-sided dice and pips (e.g., 3D+1). Pips accumulate mathematically; every 3 pips convert into a full die.
Combat resolves through opposed rolls:
The Roll: The player's assembled Damage Total is rolled against the enemy's Resistance Total.
Hidden Rolls: The engine rolls the enemy's resistance behind the scenes. This generates mathematical variance,
providing the LLM (gemma 4) with unpredictable margins of success or failure to describe narratively.
Margin of Success: The difference between the damage roll and the resistance roll dictates the severity of the consequence,
advancing the target's Wound or Fatigue tracks accordingly.
Base melee damage scales off physical attributes. The engine calculates this by converting the character's strength to pips,
dividing the number by two, rounding up and coverting back to dice plus pips. The weapon's static bonus is then added to this base.


Data-Driven Math Engine
Mechanical formulas are not hardcoded in Python. They are stored as strings directly in the TOML files (e.g., scaling_formula = "ceil(strength_dice / 2)").
AST Execution: The engine uses Python's built-in Abstract Syntax Tree (ast) module to safely parse and evaluate these formula strings at runtime,
injecting the character's current stats into the evaluation context.
Validation: During boot, the Data App evaluates every formula in the TOML files against a dummy character sheet.
It flags syntax errors or bad math in the console before the game starts, preventing crashes during gameplay.
Push-Your-Luck Mechanics
Physical techniques and spells carry inherent risk, tied to the Fatigue (FP) and Magic (MP) tracks.


Execution: Every technique and spell has an assigned difficulty number. When used, the action always succeeds mechanically and narratively.
The Cost: The player rolls a skill against the action's difficulty. If the roll meets or exceeds the difficulty, the exertion is handled perfectly.
If the roll is lower than the difficulty, the respective FP or MP track advances by 1.

Tag Engine
Conditions and elemental types are handled via text tags.
Durations: Prefixes dictate when a tag expires. Fleeting: tags expire after one action.
Scene: tags clear at the end of combat. Rest: tags persist until a long break.
Elements: Weapons and spells carry damage tags (e.g., Slashing, Fire) that trigger elemental vulnerabilities or resistances on target creatures.
Deferred Design Decisions: Rules for tag stacking (e.g., multiple "Burning" tags)
and the mechanical consequences of reaching the absolute maximum limit on the FP or MP tracks are pending future specification.
